VLA policies on the SO-101 arm, sim to real

I am building the whole learning pipeline for a low-cost robot arm myself: simulated task, scripted expert, demonstrations, policy training and evaluation, and a real leader–follower arm pair for real data. The simulation and the real arm share one interface, so a policy can move between them unchanged.

Robot
SO-101, 6× STS3215 servos, leader + follower
Stack
MuJoCo, mink, Gymnasium, LeRobot, PyTorch
Compute
RTX 3070 Ti laptop GPU
Links
Code · Notes (PDF)

Task

Pick a 4 cm cube and place it in a bin. The policy sees one fixed camera image (96×128) and the 6 joint angles, and outputs 6 joint-position targets at 30 Hz; physics runs at 480 Hz. Success is the environment's own test: the cube rests inside the bin, below the rim. I checked the success function by hand on 10 cases before trusting it.

Pipeline

  1. Simulation. The official SO-101 model, extended in code with MuJoCo's MjSpec (cube, bin, cameras) and wrapped as a Gymnasium environment. The real arm is later wrapped to the same interface.
  2. Scripted expert. A waypoint state machine driving differential inverse kinematics, solved as a small quadratic program at every step (mink) with joint limits as hard constraints. The SO-101 has 5 arm joints and cannot reach every 6-D pose, so position is weighted high, gripper tilt moderately, and wrist roll follows the cube's yaw.
  3. Demonstrations with DART. The robot executes the expert's action plus correlated noise, but the label is the clean action. This shows the policy how to recover from states the expert never visits. 8 parallel workers; only successful episodes kept. 200 episodes = 31,078 frames.
  4. Training. LeRobot's ACT policy: ResNet-18 backbone with ImageNet weights, CVAE, action chunks of 50, 51.6M parameters. The whole dataset sits on the GPU and chunks are cut by index arithmetic, with no DataLoader.
  5. Evaluation. 50 episodes on fixed unseen seeds. Every failure is sorted into a type (never grasped, pushed, dropped far) and every rate is reported with its 95% confidence interval.
Expert quality

100% success without noise and 95% with DART noise (σ = 1.5°), checked before any demonstration was recorded.

What the data supports
  • More demonstrations help: 25 → 34%, 50 → 48%, 100–200 → 62–78%.
  • Replanning every 10–25 steps beats executing the whole 50-step chunk. Every 5 steps is worse (48%): the chunk loses its smoothness.
  • Longer training helps: 62% at 6k steps vs 74% at 10k steps, both with 200 demos.
What it does not support

Ranking 100 against 200 demos, DART on against off (62% vs 50%), or chunk 50 against 20 (62% vs 56%). These gaps are inside the noise of 50 episodes and a single training seed. The next round uses 100 episodes and 3 training seeds.

Real hardware: SO-101 bring-up

I own a leader and a follower arm. Before collecting any real data I wrote a 28-item checklist in 7 groups (motors and limits, mapping, sim match, timing and data definition, camera and scene, safety, verification). The items most likely to break a real policy are the joint mapping, the timing, and the camera.

  • Motor IDs and bus settings read and recorded for all 6 servos.
  • Raw motor ticks mapped to simulator joint angles, with sign and zero per joint, checked by hand against a live simulator view.
  • Gripper mapping (closed → 0, open → 100) and the meaning of state vs action fixed: state is the measured position, action is the goal position.
  • Safety: software joint limits 40 ticks inside the measured hard stops, steps ≤ 0.5° per cycle, abort above 55 °C, arm clamped with clear space.
  • Geometry check: the tip lands 0.7 cm short of a 20 cm target (sag); base offset still to measure.

Pitfalls I hit

  • The offscreen renderer silently used the integrated GPU, so the RTX GPU sat idle during data generation. Measure before optimizing.
  • ACT's backbone uses frozen BatchNorm, so training it from scratch does not normalize; pretrained weights matter.
  • Grasp slip came from the friction cone and impratio settings, and soft contacts hid collisions. Simulator settings decide whether demonstrations are physically honest.

Next

  • Collect real demonstrations by teleoperation with the leader arm.
  • Train ACT on real data, then fine-tune a VLA (SmolVLA) and compare it against ACT on the same evaluation.
