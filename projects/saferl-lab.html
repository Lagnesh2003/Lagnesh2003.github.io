saferl-lab: sample-efficient RL for real robots

The goal: robots that learn hard physical skills in minutes on real hardware, without breaking themselves. The software half compares RL algorithms against classical control in simulation under a strict protocol. The hardware half is a home-built Furuta pendulum (and later a hopping leg) where those methods get tested for real.

Task
Furuta pendulum swing-up + balance, 100 Hz, torque ≤ 0.15 N·m
Methods
PPO, SAC, LQR, iLQR + TVLQR, energy swing-up
Simulation
MuJoCo (CPU) and MuJoCo Warp (GPU), same physics

Evaluation protocol

Every number on this page uses the same protocol: 10 training seeds, one evaluator with 100 fixed start states and deterministic actions, the interquartile mean (IQM, the mean of the middle 50% of seeds) with a 95% bootstrap confidence interval, and six robustness conditions. Before I fixed the protocol, my conclusions flipped three times.

What took PPO from 3/10 to 10/10 seeds

  • An observation gap. The arm angle was given only as cos/sin, which repeat every turn, so the policy could not see it was winding the arm toward its 2-turn limit. Fix: add the unwrapped angle.
  • A “quit” loophole in the reward. Ending the episode cost the same as hanging still, so winding the arm into the limit was never worse than doing nothing; 7 of 10 seeds settled on hanging still. Fix: hanging earns 0 and upright earns +1, so ending is clearly the worst option.
  • Simulator blow-ups that looked like success. CPU MuJoCo resets an unstable simulation to its default pose, upright and at rest, which scored as a perfect balance. Fix: detect blow-ups identically on CPU and GPU and penalize them.
  • Training details. Bootstrapping at time-outs, domain randomization, a constant learning rate and a small entropy bonus.
Caveat

These fixes went in together, so the share of each one is not measured.

Diagnosis

I probed the critics on states near upright. Holding still versus a slight wobble was worth about 0.05 out of a value of about 90, while the two critics disagreed by about 0.3: critic noise was 5–10× the signal. With the pendulum perfectly still, where the correct action is 0, the critics preferred +0.57, −1.0 and −0.73, so the policy kicked a balanced pendulum. Lower target entropy, an action-smoothness penalty, and observation and reward normalization did not fix it.

Fix: pay for stillness, then fix the critic

Environment v4 pays half of the upright reward only for holding still, so stillness is worth about 40 in value instead of about 0.1. PPO learned it. SAC still did not, until LayerNorm in the critics: the bottleneck was critic accuracy, not the reward alone.

Realistic return ceiling on v4 is about 450 (the swing-up itself costs about 50). Tuned PPO holds the pendulum essentially motionless in 9 of 10 seeds; SAC with LayerNorm is reliable at about 15× fewer samples but still wobbles more.

Longer training is not better

6 seeds × 450M steps: the best seeds reach 482 (vs 479), but 2 of 6 drifted after peaking around 20M steps, to a swing-up that leaves the arm 1.2–1.4 turns from home. Exploration noise had collapsed to σ ≈ 0.015, and γ = 0.99 (about a 1 s horizon) hides the slow arm cost. Final IQM success fell to 75% [25, 100]. Budget set to 20M steps.

Hardware: building the real pendulum

Simulation results only matter if they survive a real robot, so I am building one, on a budget under ₹1 lakh for the year. Every purchase is logged in a bill of materials with its price and the reason for each choice.

Done so far

  • Electronics chosen and ordered: T-Motor GB4106 gimbal motor, two MT6835 21-bit magnetic encoders, a SimpleFOC Mini driver and a Teensy 4.1. The motor's 15.3 Ω winding means even a stall at 24 V draws 1.57 A, which the driver's 2.5 A peak cannot exceed.
  • Safety hardware: current-limited bench supply, 3 A slow-blow fuse, latching e-stop. The firmware layer comes before any learning: torque limit, arm-angle limit, 50 ms watchdog.
  • First firmware: an exact 1 kHz control loop on the Teensy, and a 6→64→64→1 MLP policy running in 77 µs, so a 64×64 network fits inside the loop with room left for motor control.
  • Mechanical design: 9 printed parts and an audit script with 71 checks. The audit forced real fixes: motor hole spacing taken from the manufacturer's STEP file instead of my caliper, and a 0.5 mm wall thickened to 2 mm.
  • Parts in production: PETG prints ordered on 1 Nov (₹2,043, about 2% of the budget); bearings and fasteners ordered.

Next

  • Assemble and safety-test the rig, then record a build video.
  • System identification (inertia, friction, motor constant) and an updated simulation model, with a sim-vs-real plot.
  • Gate: LQR balances the real pendulum for at least 60 s.
  • Sim-to-real: a domain-randomized policy swings up and balances on hardware.
  • Gate: the pendulum learns swing-up from scratch on hardware in under 10 minutes of robot time.

Next in software

  • Higher update-to-data ratios for SAC (DroQ-style: dropout and LayerNorm critics).
  • SafeFast, a model-based method: an ensemble of learned dynamics models as a residual on physics, an MPPI planner, and a safety filter with an LQR backup controller.
